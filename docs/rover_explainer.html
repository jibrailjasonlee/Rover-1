A pin is only ever on or off. To get "half speed", flip it on and off very fast and spend half the time on. The motor feels the average.










Each motor has two sensors, A and B, slightly out of step. When A switches on, your interrupt checks B. B high means one direction, B low means the other.

Not built yet


    

  • readDistance() is an empty stub. The ultrasonic sensor is wired in the plan but nothing reads it, so there is no obstacle avoidance yet.
    • 

  • No failsafe. If the phone disconnects, the last goal stays set and the robot keeps driving. A timeout that zeroes the goals fixes that.
    • 

  • Units and gains are placeholders. Ticks per second is not yet RPM, and Kp, Ki, Kd must be tuned on the real robot.
    • 

  • No integral limit. If a wheel is held still, the integral keeps growing. That is called windup.



Say it to a friend in 30 seconds


"My robot has a tiny website on it. I tap Forward on my phone, and the robot writes down how fast each wheel should go. Twenty times a second it checks how fast the wheels are actually turning, using sensors that count shaft ticks, and nudges the power up or down to close the gap. That checking and correcting is the whole trick, and it's why two slightly different motors still drive in a straight line."
